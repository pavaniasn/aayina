I used to love the road —
the faces, the chaos, the endless skies.
But now, something has shifted.
The streets I once dreamt of,
the city on my childhood bucket list,
leave me hollow.

Deep down, I know why,
but I can't let it out —
saying it would make it real.

You, sitting at my cramped kitchen table.
You, with your laughter, your silence,
and our endless conversations,
turned a place so foreign, so alien,
into something that feels closest to home.

You are the harbor for the wanderer in me,
the quiet I never knew I needed.

But I cannot stay.
No matter how much I wish I could,
this home is not mine to keep.

Unwillingly, I choose the road again —
not because I want to,
but because I have to.

So, I leave behind the joy I found
in our simplest conversations,
and carry with me the weight of what could never be.
